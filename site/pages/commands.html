/aeom judge

Judge your front end.

When before you change anything.

  • Reads your product, then replays its key journeys in a real browser, on phone and desktop.
  • Measures every screen: contrast, clickable elements, sideways scroll, console errors, the header on scroll, text that wraps or gets cut, tap targets, text size, line length, zoom, images, accessible names, keyboard focus, layout shift.
  • Holds each screen to eight design principles, each journey to six of its own.
  • Says what fails, in plain words. Changes nothing.

/aeom directions

See where it could go.

When you want to see other art directions, before anything is rebuilt.

  • Starts from your product as it is, a prompt of one line, or a spec.
  • Draws three art directions from your product's own world, each a mockup of two of your screens with what they hold.
  • Runs the checks on each, and a judge says what it sees beside each. None is left out: you choose.
  • Touches nothing in your project: no branch, no line of your code.
  • Keeps the one you choose, for /aeom redo to carry into your app, or /aeom build to build a front on.

/aeom redo

Redo your front end.

When the judge finds it falls short.

  • Says what the run will cost, waits for your go, then judges like /aeom judge.
  • Draws three art directions from your product's own world, and you pick one.
  • Rebuilds every screen and journey on it, keeps only what got better, never adds a feature.
  • Writes the standard of your front end, and opens a pull request.

/aeom review

Check a pull request.

When before you merge, whoever wrote it.

  • Holds the screens it touches to the standard of your front end.
  • Flags a colour or a font outside its tokens, a component redone by hand, a different header.
  • Checks the journeys it crosses still reach their end.
  • Gives a clear verdict, as a GitHub check.

/aeom build

Mock up a front end.

When you have a spec or an idea, and no front yet.

  • Reads your spec, or a prompt of one line, and drafts the product sheet and its key journeys from it.
  • Says what the run will cost, and waits for your go.
  • Draws three art directions from your product's own world, and you pick one.
  • Mocks up the screens of each key journey on it: front only, on an API running on your machine or on fake data named as such.
  • Replays each journey to its end, writes the standard of your front end, and opens a pull request.
