The knockout, three judges a duel

  1. Round 1

    Press proof beat Eye chart, 3–0

    Print-proof frame with CMYK bar, crop marks and a BAT approval box; proofreader circles act out judging screens. Eye-chart shrinking type makes later steps hard to read and repeats 4/10.

    All three reasons
    • Print-proof frame with CMYK bar, crop marks and a BAT approval box; proofreader circles act out judging screens. Eye-chart shrinking type makes later steps hard to read and repeats 4/10.
    • Crop marks line up with each card, CMYK colors tag every step and command, problems circled like proofreader marks; eye-chart section counters all read 4/10 and its Now row sticks out of the column.
    • BAT sign-off box, proofreader circles on the faults, crop marks around aligned cards; eye-chart shrinking headings make WAVES and RATCHET hard to read and its red and green blocks take over the title.
  2. Round 1

    Light table beat Tailor fitting, 3–0

    Contact sheet of wireframe screens crossed out in red except the one circled in grease pencil shows the judge-and-keep loop; tailor-fitting charcoal with yellow tape accents drifts toward near-black plus amber.

    All three reasons
    • Contact sheet of wireframe screens crossed out in red except the one circled in grease pencil shows the judge-and-keep loop; tailor-fitting charcoal with yellow tape accents drifts toward near-black plus amber.
    • Film strip of thumbnails crossed out except the circled keeper, same circle marks Now on the roadmap, one filled Star button; tailor-fitting only says measuring, two equal dashed buttons, dark plus yellow palette.
    • Hero contact sheet where every rejected take is crossed out and the kept frame circled, one focal point under the dot-matrix title; tailor-fitting leans toward dark screen with one amber accent.
  3. Round 1

    Reflectography beat Judges board, 3–0

    Hero splits one screen into visible and infrared and circles three real agent mistakes on a lettered grid; judges-board pool metaphor needs explaining and its six boxed step cards look like a template.

    All three reasons
    • Hero splits one screen into visible and infrared and circles three real agent mistakes on a lettered grid; judges-board pool metaphor needs explaining and its six boxed step cards look like a template.
    • Annotated figure with A-D grid and a visible-to-infrared slider, magenta callouts mark what AEOM catches; judges-board pool tiles and lane ropes are mostly decoration apart from its judge card.
    • Visible and infrared views with mistakes at grid coordinates explain the product at a glance; judges-board six bordered cards and slanted yellow tags on a navy-and-yellow grid look like a template.
  4. Round 2

    Light table beat Press proof, 3–0

    Contact-sheet hero with five captures crossed out and one circled shows the product at work, carried by film strips, slide mounts and the circled Now; press-proof is cleaner but a stock print-proof look over six white cards and a table.

    All three reasons
    • Contact-sheet hero with five captures crossed out and one circled shows the product at work, carried by film strips, slide mounts and the circled Now; press-proof is cleaner but a stock print-proof look over six white cards and a table.
    • Contact sheet of app screens crossed out with one circled in grease pencil, carried through How it works, Commands and Roadmap; press-proof CMYK bars, crop marks and BAT box with a heavy wide grotesk are a stock print-shop look.
    • Film strip where every frame is crossed out except one circled keeper makes the judge-and-keep loop clear before reading; press-proof wraps a plain big-grotesque text hero that shows nothing of the product in CMYK bars and crop marks.

    Reflectography goes through without a duel.

  6. Final

    Reflectography beat Light table, 3–0

    Split visible and infrared screen with numbered marks on the three things AEOM catches and a lettered column ruler keeps a clear hierarchy; light-table dot-matrix title and heavy film-strip frames crowd the copy and put the metaphor ahead of the product.

    All three reasons
    • Split visible and infrared screen with numbered marks on the three things AEOM catches and a lettered column ruler keeps a clear hierarchy; light-table dot-matrix title and heavy film-strip frames crowd the copy and put the metaphor ahead of the product.
    • Annotated visible/infrared figure on an A-L grid marks the exact failures AEOM catches, held by a ruled method list, hatched legend and layered roadmap; light-table wraps every section, even the install code, in film perforations and tells a developer less.
    • Numbered magenta marks show the exact failures AEOM catches and the conservation-lab language holds throughout with one focal point and calm density; light-table dot-matrix title, abstract thumbnails, CTA below the strip and heavy perforations make it louder and harder to read.
