# Journey principles

What the judge asks of each key journey, on top of the base principles that each page answers on its own. Plain text, no images.

Each principle has an id in backticks, then what it asks for. The judge looks at a journey's captures in order, step by step, at every width, and answers pass or fail for each principle, with one sentence on what the user lives and the step where it shows.

A journey the app does not let the user finish is reported by `aeom journey` on its own; the judge still says what the steps before the break show.

- `clear-start` — The user sees where to start: the journey's first action is visible on the screen they start from, without scrolling or guessing, and named in their words.
- `next-step` — At every step, what to do next is visible and named. The user never has to hunt for a link at the bottom of a page or press something that does not look pressable.
- `result-shown` — When the journey ends, the screen shows its outcome: what was added, sent or found. An empty result says it is empty and what to do next; a blank area or a silent click is a failure.
- `no-dead-end` — No step leaves the user stuck: an error, a missing page or a control that does nothing offers a way on or a way back.
- `short` — The journey takes no more steps than its goal needs, and never sends the user back to a screen they already left.
- `same-words` — What the user is after keeps one name all along the journey: the link, the title and the button agree, in the same language.
