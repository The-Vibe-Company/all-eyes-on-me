# Base principles

The principles every AEOM install starts from. Plain text, no images.

Each principle has an id in backticks, then what it asks for. The judge answers pass or fail for each one, on each page, and says why in one sentence.

This is a draft. It gets settled in a dedicated session, then grows with each person's feedback in their personal layer. What `not-generic` names beyond its first sentence, and four of the checks below, come from the bans of [impeccable](https://impeccable.style) (Apache-2.0).

What a machine can measure is not here: a pointer cursor on every clickable element, no sideways scrolling, AA contrast, a clean console, a header that stays when you scroll, labels on one line, rows of cards of one shape, no text cut without an ellipsis, targets big enough to tap, text big enough to read, lines short enough to follow, zoom left to people, images that load and keep their shape, a name for every button, link, field and image, a visible keyboard focus, a page that does not move while it loads, no text filled with a gradient, no box marked by a coloured stripe on one side, no card inside a card, no motion that bounces, no hole left unfilled, no text about the interface or the demo, no hollow word, no word alone on a heading's last line, an inner radius that follows its corner, motion with a curve of its own that never moves the layout, and a finished page (its title, an icon, a skip link, a description and a share image, a 404 that leads back) are checked by `aeom check`. The judge looks at screenshots, so these principles are about what a screenshot shows.

- `one-direction` — One art direction, held on every page. No page borrows another style.
- `consistent-chrome` — The header and the navigation are the same on every page: same place, same look, same order. Buttons share one style everywhere, wherever a page needs them.
- `not-generic` — Nothing generic: no purple-to-blue gradient, no shadowed cards everywhere, no decorative emoji, no layout that could be any SaaS template. Nor the SaaS reflexes: a big number with a small label and stats around it as the page's hero, a grid of identical cards (an icon, a heading, a line of text, again and again), frosted glass used as decoration. Nor a palette and a theme anyone could guess from the kind of product alone: observability in dark blue, health in white and teal, finance in navy and gold, crypto in neon on black.
- `not-ai-default` — It does not look like what an AI proposes when asked to be bold: cream or newsprint paper with a big serif and a red or terracotta accent; a near-black screen with one amber, green or phosphor accent in monospace; newspaper rules and columns; a white Swiss poster with a red square; a black-and-yellow brutalist grid; Didot and small caps with a gold accent; kraft paper and stamps; a purple-to-blue gradient; Inter or Space Grotesk on white with rounded cards. If one of these combinations describes the page as a whole, it fails; a single trait, such as a cream background or a serif headline, does not.
- `hierarchy` — A clear hierarchy, with one focal point per page. The eye knows where to start.
- `grid` — Elements line up on a grid. Edges, gutters and baselines agree.
- `density` — Density is controlled: nothing cramped, nothing lost in empty space.
- `states` — The state the page shows is designed: an empty list says it is empty and what to do next, an error says what went wrong. A blank area where content should be is a failure.
