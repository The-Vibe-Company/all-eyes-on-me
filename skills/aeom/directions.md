# Directions

Three directions for one product by default, or as many as the user asks: not one of them may look like an AI default, and no two may stand on one skeleton. `/aeom directions` adds one exception, the classic (**The classic one** below).

## The banned looks

A model asked to "be bold" reaches for these. A direction that one of them describes as a whole is named, whatever its quality: when the person chooses, beside it, and they decide; with `--autonome`, the screening judges remove it before the bracket, so two banned directions can never meet in a duel. One trait alone does not make a banned look; a cream background or a serif headline can be a real brand choice. It is the combination that gives the default away.

- Cream or newsprint paper, a big serif headline, a red, brick or terracotta accent.
- A near-black screen with one amber, acid green or phosphor accent, in monospace.
- Newspaper hairlines, magazine columns, drop caps, pull quotes.
- A white Swiss poster: a huge grotesque headline and one red square.
- A black-and-yellow brutalist grid with heavy borders.
- Didot or another high-contrast serif with small caps and a gold or brass accent.
- Kraft paper, rubber stamps, scalloped edges, hand-drawn labels.
- A purple-to-blue gradient, glassy cards, glow.
- Inter or Space Grotesk on white with rounded cards, but for the classic of `/aeom directions`.
- A card, a callout or a row marked by a coloured bar along one side or along its top, the rounded corners bending it at both ends: an accent stripe standing in for a design. `aeom check`'s side-stripe names it, whether the bar is a border, an inset shadow or a pseudo-element.
- A dated look, the web of the 2000s and early 2010s: glossy or bevelled buttons, a gradient on every block, heavy borders and drop shadows on everything, clip-art pictograms, rounded bubbly type. A direction must look like what a good team would ship this year.
- On a game or a creative product, a document interface laid over it: a survey map with contour lines and a graduated frame, an administrative form, a dashboard of cards and charts, a spreadsheet. Players open a game to enter its world, not to read a document about it.

Design movements are banned as starting points for the same reason: "Swiss", "brutalist", "editorial", "retro terminal", "luxury" and "artisanal" are exactly where these looks come from.

## Where a direction comes from

**The product's name may give a wink, never the look.** A detail that makes sense, once, and only when it comes naturally from the direction's own source: the eye in the O of All Eyes On Me, a fleet in formation in the hero of Armada, a nod to Star Wars for a tool that runs a fleet of coding agents. These are examples of the kind, not winks to reuse. The look itself comes from the world of the people who use the product: Armada's users are developers who run agents, so its world is the review and the merge (a diff read line by line, a queue of pull requests, a run that turns green), not a navy, signal flags or a sky full of planes, and not a phosphor terminal either, which is a banned look. A direction drawn from the name alone is a costume: drop it.

From the product's own world, not from design history. That world is the product brief, `aeom product --brief`: what kind of product it is, what the app is for, who uses it, its main loop, and for a game or a creative product its world. A tool's world is the real one its users live in; a game's is its fiction. The steps below are for a tool; a game or a creative product follows **For a game or a creative product** further down.

1. **List twice as many sources as directions** (six for three) that the people the brief names would recognise, around what the app is for and what they do again and again, and that are not websites: an object they handle, a document they receive, a sign they read, a place they go, a trade that serves them, a tool, a package, a form. Each one says, in one sentence, which part of the brief it serves and how. What the brief leaves out as to confirm is a guess: no source rests on it. When it leaves out who uses the app, start from what it is for and its loop. Find new ones each time, for this product.
2. **Pick as many as directions** that are far apart in colour, type, density and material. Drop any source whose obvious rendering is one of the banned looks. Mix kinds of source: no more than one printed document for every three directions (forms, labels, manuals, plans all end up as white paper and a technical sans), and at least one source that is not on paper at all, such as a material, a place, a light, a sound turned visible, a gesture of the trade. Far apart in light too: at most one source whose colours are greys, slate or navy, and at least one that brings a saturated colour or a strong light. Sources of one family, such as the coast of one region (its sky, its stone, its harbour), are one source told three times: they come out as three greys. When the person gives the sources and they are of one family, say so before drawing, with a far one to put beside them; they decide.
3. **Give each worker its source, its sentence and its structure** (**A structure of its own** below). It takes what makes the source recognisable (its colour system, its type, its layout conventions, its marks and codes) and turns that into a web kit. It does not draw a picture of the object.

For a shop selling lamps, chairs and rugs, a first run picked a paint chip fan deck, the European energy label, an assembly sheet, a carbon-copy order form, a furnished floor plan and a 1975 mail-order catalogue. Five of the six were printed documents, and five came out as white paper with a technical sans: only the catalogue stood apart. Sources like the glow of a lampshade at dusk, a wool rug's dyed yarn on the loom, or a chair maker's bench with its oiled wood would have spread the six further. These are examples of the kind, not a list to reuse.

## For a game or a creative product

Players do not live in the real world of the things a game names: they live in its fiction and in its genre. A strategy game about explorers and wild beasts is not a hiking trail, a survey map or a camping tent, even though explorers walk trails and read maps. A first run on such a game drew six directions from the real outdoors (trail markers, undergrowth, a topographic map, a bivouac tent, a decorated cave, morning mist); the map won every duel, and the game came out looking like a printed survey sheet, with its painted beasts pasted onto it.

1. **List twice as many sources as directions, from all four of these kinds**, each with its sentence naming the part of the brief it serves:
   - **the fiction**: a place, a people, a ritual, an object of that world as the game tells it (the chief's hut at dusk, the totems of a tribe, a tamer's camp, a bestiary kept by the tribe);
   - **the illustration style of the visual assets**: their palette, their light, their finish, their outline, taken further into a whole interface;
   - **the interface conventions of the genre**: how the games of its references show resources, collections, inventories, cards, maps and timers, and how they put the interface inside the world (a counter carved in wood, a card framed like the beast's portrait);
   - **the materials and lights of that world**: what its things are made of and how they are lit, as the illustrations show it (oiled wood and leather, firelight, golden grass at sunset).

   These are examples of the kind, not a list to reuse: find the sources of this game, in its own brief.
2. **The visual assets are a constraint, not a source among others.** Every direction must make the existing illustrations and logo look at home: same family of light and colour, frames that suit them, nothing that makes them look pasted on. A direction that would clash with them is dropped, however good it looks alone. Name in each sentence what the direction keeps of them.
3. **Pick as many as directions**, far apart in colour, type, density and material, as for a tool, spread over as many kinds as there are directions, up to all four. Drop any whose obvious rendering is a banned look, the document interface first.
4. **Give each worker its source, its sentence, its structure (**A structure of its own** below) and the visual assets** as the brief lists them, with the paths of a few to look at: it opens them before it builds.

## A structure of its own

A source alone gives three skins on one skeleton: the same header, the same search bar, the same cards in the same places, only the colours and the type changing. So each direction gets a structure too, its thesis, and no two directions of a round share one. The source decides the look; the thesis decides the skeleton.

1. **One thesis per direction**, in one plain sentence beside its source's: where the main action sits, how the content is laid out, how one goes from screen to screen (a bar at the top, a rail at the side, tabs at the bottom, none). Such as the image first and the action under it; a workbench in panes, the list on one side and the record open beside it; a timeline one scrolls through; one column read from top to bottom like a letter; a board whose columns the work moves across; one action in front and the rest folded away. These are examples of the kind, not a list to reuse: draw each from its source and from what the users do most, as a counter's ticket rail gives a queue read from left to right, or a lectern one document in the middle with its notes in the margin.
2. **None the same.** Two theses that would draw the same skeleton, such as two grids of cards under a header, are one: change one. On the product as it is, none is its current structure: a direction that keeps its header, its menu and its blocks where they are is the front repainted.
3. **What a screen holds stays**: every piece of data and every action, laid out anew. A thesis never drops an action to make room, nor adds one.
4. **A charter that fixes the look** keeps it whole in every direction: the theses are then what sets them apart. Without one, the product's own colour, its logo's, comes back as a touch at most, never as the palette.

## A direction that dares

Steering clear of the banned looks is not a direction. A round where every direction is grey, slate or navy, in one condensed sans, with colour only on a badge, has avoided every cliché and still says nothing: a person who sees ten proposals, a client or the jury of a tender, has forgotten it by evening. Every direction but the classic takes a real risk, drawn from its source:

- **A signature**: on the first screen, one thing no other product of its kind shows, that one could describe from memory the next day, such as a garden shop's plants laid across the page from deep shade to full sun, as they need light, or a rowing club's crews set as the lanes of a race. These are examples of the kind, not signatures to reuse. It comes from the source and carries the main loop: a way of showing the data or the action, never a decoration set beside it.
- **A colour that owns space**: at least one colour of the source in large areas, a band, a field, a whole panel, not only on a badge or a button. Greys with one accent are the classic's palette. In the calm register, one band at most, tinted rather than saturated.
- **A type with a voice**: a typeface chosen for its character, from the source, for the titles and the figures. No two directions of a round share a family.
- **Contrast of scale**: what the user came for, a figure, a level, a deadline, set far bigger than the rest. A screen where everything is one size reads as a form. One figure per screen: when every number is huge, none is.

**In the calm register, one strong gesture.** A work tool, a back office or a dashboard read every day dares once, in how its main data is drawn, and nowhere else: one band of colour at most, one figure set large, a voice in the titles only. A full band, a huge figure, a coloured field and a heavy rule on one screen read as shouting, and a jury reads that as a draft. **What comes first**, when they pull apart: the spec, then the person's taste and the register of the kind, then this section. A dare that crowds the first screen, hides a requirement or raises the voice of a calm tool goes; the direction keeps its source in its colour, type and composition.

Daring is never ornament. No geometry drawn for the source's sake: a polygon, a cone or a beam of light, a halo, a rosette, a dial; no object of the source drawn to carry the content, such as a plate or a lighthouse holding the forecast. The source gives the colours, the type and the rhythm; the content keeps the shape of its kind. Dense screens dare too: a table takes the direction's colour, type and rhythm. A dense screen that is the classic's with another header has not dared. Daring is in how a thing is shown, never in what is hidden: what the brief keeps stays whole, readable, and every level written out; contrast is measured by `aeom check`.

## Pictures

What a screen shows of the real world is shown as it is. A real place, building, landscape, species, crop, object or event, such as a town and its port, a museum's collection or a field in spring, is a photograph of it under a free licence, from Wikimedia Commons (public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA), found through its API with a generic User-Agent and only the thing it shows as the words of the query. Its author and its licence are written beside it on the screen, and kept with its address in `credits.md` beside the images. A photograph stands beside the content it shows, never as decoration: a kind that shows none, such as a weather home that leads with the weather or a work tool, has none, and its pictograms and charts carry it. Never a picture drawn in CSS or SVG, never a landscape in flat shapes, never a generated image of a real place, never a fake logo: CSS and SVG draw icons, charts and maps of data only. A generated image, `aeom image`, is for a product that draws its own world, such as a game or a brand that illustrates, in that world's illustration style.

## For a tender

`/aeom directions --tender` is for a call for tenders, a pitch or a brief answered with mockups: a jury compares them with other bids, in a few minutes each, and buys what it can picture in production. The directions must sell, so they are different from each other without going in every direction:

- **Its kind at a glance.** A weather site looks like a weather site: today's weather and the days ahead first, the warning, the town; a work tool looks like a work tool. A jury that has to be told what it looks at has already moved on. A direction's structure is one of its kind's known patterns (a top bar, a side rail, a board, a list and its record), each direction a different one; never a radial page, a hidden main menu or a layout that must be learnt.
- **Layered reading.** The first screen, at the widest width and 800 px high, without scrolling, shows a sentence of state, two or three key figures, what waits for the user and the main action; nothing else competes for the eye. Every requirement of the spec keeps one summary line in sight, a word of state and a figure, such as « Sauvegarde : réussie, cette nuit à 2 h 10 »; its detail (lists, histories, definitions, secondary measures) opens on a click, or in a tooltip on hover and focus. It folds without a script: `<details>` and `<summary>` for a line that opens on its detail, the `popover` attribute with a `popovertarget` button for a panel, and for a short definition a tooltip in CSS shown on `:hover`, `:focus-visible` and `:focus-within`, with `role="tooltip"` and the trigger's `aria-describedby`. What is folded stays in the HTML, read by a screen reader, and a discreet cue says there is more: a chevron, « Détails », a dotted underline. One fold only is open by default on each page, the one that serves most, so the capture shows how it opens. A page shows 400 words at most before anything is opened; `aeom check`'s layers counts them. A round refused whole showed everything at once, small, boxed and with nothing to open; the next said more, and folded it.
- **The register of its kind.** Decide it once from the brief, before drawing, and give it to every worker. A work tool, a back office, a public service's forms, a dashboard read every day is **calm**: neutrals, one accent, one or two families of type, colour only where it means something; its daring is in how its main data is drawn, such as the week's deadlines on one strip, the day read as a timetable or the search set as the whole head of the page, never in a palette or a motif. A product the public opens for a subject that is itself alive, such as the weather, a festival, a museum or a coast, is **lively**, and its life comes from its data and its real world: the colour of the sky behind today's weather, a temperature scale on every bar, the photograph of the place. A town's site sits between: calm, with the town in its photographs. A jury buys a calm tool and a lively subject; it never buys a tool in costume, nor a dull weather.
- **One mark, where it serves.** The direction's mark, an arrow, a stripe, a highlight, a stamp, a colour code, appears where it carries the data or the main action. Repeated on every button, every row, every heading or the edge of every block, it is ornament: an arrow on each button, a flag before each line, a highlighter under each title, capitals on each label, a ruler along the page. In the calm register, the colour that owns space is one band at most, such as the head of the page or its search.
- **Pictures of the real world, or none**, as **Pictures** above says: a town's site shows the town, a museum its collection, each photograph credited beside it; a weather home or a work tool shows none.
- **The difference is in colour, type, density and composition**, and in how the main data is shown: the days as bands, a deadline set huge, the warning as a whole field. **A direction that dares** holds, and its ban on ornament first: nothing drawn, no texture, no metaphor of the source made literal.
- **The level of the best of its kind.** Before drawing, look at four to six of the best products of the kind, and say what makes them good: how the main figure is set, how the data is drawn, the icons, the spacing, the type sizes. Beside them, one or two very finished product interfaces of any kind, for their finish alone: few words a line, secondary text in grey, thin pictograms, one thing put forward, lists with no frame around each row, a lot of air. The products of the kind give the genre, the finished ones the level. A direction reaches that level, never their look: no layout, colour or brand taken from one of them.
- **Its kind's icons and charts.** A kind that lives by pictograms gets a real icon set, consistent and under an open licence, its licence kept beside the pages: a weather site its sky pictograms, a shop its product photos. Its data is drawn the way its users already read it, such as an hour-by-hour strip, a high and low range bar, rain as bars. These are not ornament: the ban on ornament is about decoration, never about the pictograms and charts of the kind.
- **Readable first.** Running text at 15 or 16 px, in two sizes at most, never under 13 px; numbers that carry the screen far bigger; at least 20 px between two rows of a list and 48 to 64 px between two sections; few frames, a hairline or a light ground setting a block apart, never a border around each block, two framed blocks at most on the first screen; secondary text in a grey of 4.5:1 at least; one focal point per screen. `aeom check`'s font-size, contrast and layers pass. Type that reads: a voice in the titles at most, figures that line up and look plain, never a slashed or dotted zero outside code, capitals for a short label only.
- **Pared down, never coarse.** A jury reads the finish before the idea. Generous margins, hairlines of 1 px rather than rules of 2 or more, one weight of line throughout, icons of one thin family, no saturated field behind running text, no block marked by a bar on one side or along its top, no badge on every row. What a serious agency would deliver as it is, never a draft: when in doubt, take away.
- **Every requirement within reach.** What the spec asks to see (dates, levels written out, deadlines, a phone number, a legal mention) keeps its summary line in sight, and its detail one click or one hover away, because the jury checks it against its grid: a direction that hides one, or folds it without its line, loses points whatever its look. A requirement is shown by the product doing it, never by quoting the spec: no article number, no « CCTP » or « RC » on a screen, no sentence that argues for the bid, such as how the figures are computed, « sans ressaisie », or « 10 engagements sur 11 tenus » set as a headline. The screen informs; the written answer argues. A number the users themselves use, such as an indicator's in its own table, stays, once; a figure is said once per screen. A colour that already means something in the product's world keeps that meaning only: where yellow, orange and red are warning levels, they never colour a rain band, a brand or a deadline.
- **Real and finished**: the spec's own data and words, every state drawn, a brand the client could replace by its own when the spec asks for it, pages that look delivered, not explored.

The sources still come from the product's world, as **Where a direction comes from** says, but each gives a palette, a type and a rhythm, never a picture. When these bullets and **A direction that dares** pull apart, the spec comes first, then the person's taste and the register of the kind, then daring. The classic is drawn as ever, held to this section like the others.

## The classic one

Every round of `/aeom directions` holds exactly one direction that takes no risk, its slug `classique`: the interface a careful team ships this year with a well-known kit such as shadcn/ui, for the person who wants a front that is clean, readable and current rather than original. Neutral greys on white, or a dark mode as sober; one accent, the product's own colour when it has one; one plain sans, such as Geist, Inter or the system's; thin borders, small radii, light shadows if any, generous spacing; the conventional skeleton of its kind, a side rail and a content area for a tool, a top bar and a grid for a shop, a top bar and one column for a page that is read. For a game or a creative product, the clean, flat interface of the best-known games of its genre, its illustrations and logo kept as they are, framed plainly.

- **No source from the product's world**: no material, no texture, no illustration style of its own, no wink at the product's name. Its sentence says what it puts forward of the product's main loop, and how.
- **Still a direction**: polished, current, every state drawn, the data and the actions of each screen laid out with care. A dated look stays banned, and so does every other banned look; « Inter or Space Grotesk on white with rounded cards » alone is its own.
- **The convention is its structure**: it is the one exception to **A structure of its own** above. It may stand on the skeleton the front as it is already follows; no other direction of the round takes that convention.
- **A charter that fixes the look** is kept whole: the classic is then the sober reading of what it leaves open.

## Saying a direction in plain words

The person chooses between directions they may never have seen named. Each direction comes back from its worker with two plain sentences: what one sees (the colours, the materials, the light, the shapes of the blocks and buttons) and what it takes from the product, in the words of someone who is not a designer. No trade words: not « neatline », « graduated frame », « bento », « brutalist », « grotesque », « editorial ». « A map border of black and white squares around each block » says what « a graduated frame » hides.
