# Archetypes

Where each of the six directions starts. A direction takes its archetype as a stance, then makes it specific to the product: its content, its audience, its words. It never copies a famous site.

1. **suisse** — International Typographic Style. A strict grid, one sans-serif in a few weights, flush-left text, generous white, one strong accent colour, information first.
2. **brutaliste** — Raw and direct. System or monospace type, visible borders, flat blocks of colour, no rounding, no softness, layout that shows its structure.
3. **editorial** — A magazine page. A characterful serif for headlines, a quiet text face, columns, pull quotes, rules, captions, a sense of reading.
4. **terminal-retro** — An old computer screen. Monospace everywhere, a dark ground with one phosphor colour, boxes drawn with lines, keyboard-first affordances.
5. **luxe** — Quiet luxury. A refined serif with high contrast, lots of space, a muted palette with one precious accent, small caps, slow rhythm.
6. **artisanal** — A craft shop. Warm natural colours, a friendly humanist face, hand-made textures suggested with simple shapes, labels and stamps.
