# /aeom: directions in mockups

Part of the `/aeom` skill: [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) says when to read this file (`/aeom directions`). From SKILL.md it needs **A run that stops, and is taken up again** and **AEOM's agents**, and for the front as it is, section 0's **The app** and **Which widths**; the product sheet is in [product.md](product.md); the banned looks, the paragraph on the product's name and the screening brief are in [directions.md](directions.md) and [phase-directions.md](phase-directions.md).

## 2d. Directions in mockups (`/aeom directions [<a sentence> | <a file>]`)

Three directions, drawn in minutes as static mockups, for the person to look at and choose, and nothing in the project touched: no run branch, no worktree, no commit, no worker in the project's code. Everything is written in `.aeom/runs/$RUN/`, but for the direction chosen, kept in `.aeom/direction/`. Speed is the point: the session reads, three workers draw at once, one command captures and checks them all, a judge says what it sees, the person chooses. The person chooses even with `--autonome`: no judge picks a direction for them, and none is left out before they do.

**The source** is one of three:
- **Nothing: the product as it is**, the front of this folder's app, started as section 0's **The app** says. Its directions redraw its own screens with what they hold: no feature added or taken away.
- **A prompt**: a sentence in quotes, or a file that says how the product should look, such as the one `/style` writes.
- **A spec**: a file that says what the product is, who uses it, its screens and its rules, a « cahier des charges ». When a file could be either, ask once.

`/aeom --depuis <file>` is `/aeom directions <file>`.

1. **Read the source**, the session alone. `RUN` = `run-<YYYYMMDD-HHMM>`, then `mkdir -p .aeom/runs/$RUN`. The charter is `.veebee/style.md` when it exists: note what it fixes (colours, type, feeling, references) and what it leaves open.
   - **The product as it is**: its product sheet, `.aeom/product.md`; when there is none, draft its brief from the code and the screens (`aeom capture --out .aeom/runs/$RUN/look`), as product.md says but without key journeys, in `.aeom/runs/$RUN/product.md`: `aeom product` asks for journeys, and the sheet of the project is a redo's to write. `WIDTHS` as section 0's **Which widths** says, the config's or `390,1280` when no sheet says where the front is used.
   - **A prompt**: written to `.aeom/runs/$RUN/prompt.md`, so a run taken up still has it. Note the product's name, what it is for, who uses it and on what, and the screens the prompt lists, each with who is on it, what they came to do and what must be visible. `WIDTHS` is `390` when it says a phone only, `1280` when it says a computer only, and `390,1280` when it says both or nothing: never a guess.
   - **A spec**: kept in `.aeom/runs/$RUN/source.md`; draft its brief as product.md says, with `(seen: <the spec's file>)`, `(to confirm)` for what it does not say, without key journeys, in `.aeom/runs/$RUN/product.md`: the sheet of the project is `/aeom build --from-scratch`'s to write. `WIDTHS` as for a prompt, from what the spec says.

   Then `aeom image --check`, once, as section 0's **Images** says: the briefs say images on or off. Mark the run: `aeom run mark .aeom/runs/$RUN --phase directions --step 2d.1 --option "directions <the source, or nothing>" --set "WIDTHS=<it>" --set "MAIN=$(pwd)"`.
2. **No cost said.** Nothing is said of what the run costs and nothing waits for a go: three workers start.
3. **Two screens**, `index.html` and `<its slug>.html` in each mockup:
   - **The product as it is**: the screen a user lands on (the config's `home`, otherwise `/`) and the densest of its main loop, a list, a form or a dashboard. Capture them as they are, at the widest of `$WIDTHS`: `aeom capture --pages <the two routes> --widths <the widest> --out .aeom/runs/$RUN/now`.
   - **A prompt or a spec**: the one a user lands on first, and the densest. One that lists one screen: that one, and the one its user opens next. None: the first screen its words describe, and the one its user works in most.
4. **Three angles**, or as many as `--directions` says. When the charter fixes the look, every direction keeps it whole, its colours, type and feeling, and they differ on what it leaves open: the composition, the density, the materials and the light, how the main action is put forward. Without a charter, draw them from the product's world, as directions.md says, with the source as the product brief. For the product as it is, none is its current look tidied up: each is a direction of its own. Give each a short slug and one sentence saying how it reads the charter, or what it is drawn from. The banned looks apply to what the charter leaves open, never against what it asks for in so many words.
5. **One worker per direction, at once**, each an `aeom-worker` agent on this session's own model, passed explicitly as **AEOM's agents** in SKILL.md says, with the mockup brief below. A variant runs on Sonnet, as step 7 says. No worktree: each writes in its own folder, `$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions/<slug>/`. Mark each as **A run that stops** says.
6. **Capture, lay out and check them all, in one command**, once every worker replied: `aeom mockups "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" <the slugs of this round> --widths "$WIDTHS" --note "<slug>=<its sentence>"...`, with, for the product as it is, its two screens first: `--now "/=<its capture>" --now "<dense route>=<its capture>"` (from `.aeom/runs/$RUN/now/manifest.json`). It captures each direction's two pages at the widest width, lays them out, checks them at every width, and lays every direction out in `directions/sheet.png`, the front as it is first; it says, for each, what its checks found. A direction a check fails on is not sent back, there is no time for it: what fails is said next to it. One whose pages do not open is left out; say so.
7. **Show them all, then ask.** One screening judge, an `aeom-judge` agent, given `directions/sheet.png` and the screening brief of phase-directions.md; from a prompt or a spec, with these changes: there is no front as it is, so it is given the source and the charter in place of the product brief, and in place of « less polished, less clear or less current than the front as it is », « less polished or less current than what a good team ships this year »; one more reason to name a direction, « it betrays what the charter fixes »; and a banned look the charter asks for in so many words is not one. What the judge names goes beside the direction, never in its place: every direction that built is shown. Show the sheet and ask, with the session's question tool when it has one: each direction in its two plain sentences, with what its checks found and what the judge said of it, and the question, which one, or none. Record it: `aeom tournament start --no-knockout --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" <the slugs>`, then `aeom tournament decide --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" --choose <slug>`, with `--why "<their words>"` when they gave any. Keep it in the project: `aeom direction keep "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions/<slug>" --sentence "<its sentence>" --source "<the front as it is, the prompt, or the spec's file>"`, with the same `--why`. None: say why, and draw three new angles from what they said, once.
   **Variants.** When the person asks for variants of a direction they like, one or several, draw each in its own folder, `directions/<slug>-v<n>/`, each an `aeom-worker` agent launched without `model`, so on Sonnet: the taste is set, the worker carries it out. Give it the mockup brief, the folder of the direction it varies and what the person asked for. Then step 6 with the variants' slugs, and this step again: show them beside the direction they come from, and ask.
8. **The end.** Say where the direction kept is, `.aeom/direction/`, whose pages a browser opens as they are, and what comes next, when the user decides it: on the product as it is, `/aeom redo` carries it into the app; from a prompt or a spec, `/aeom build --from-scratch <the same source>` builds the front on it. `aeom direction drop` forgets it. Nothing else in the project changed: commit nothing. Mark `--phase done`.

**Mockup brief.** Give the worker its folder, whether images are on, the two screens (file name, who is on it, what they came to do, what must be visible, and for the front as it is, the path of its capture), the charter and the source as they are written (the product sheet for the front as it is or a spec), its angle and its sentence, the banned looks and the paragraph on the product's name from directions.md, and the widths. Ask it to:

> Draw this direction as two static pages in plain HTML and CSS, `index.html` and `<dense>.html`, with one shared `style.css`, in your folder and nowhere else. Work fast: two pages, not a design system. No framework, no build, no script the pages need: a browser opens them as files. Fonts from Google Fonts or the system; no picture from the web. When the brief says images are on, generate the ones these screens need where an image serves, a hero, a section, an empty state, an onboarding or a header, one to three in all, with `aeom image "<what it shows, in your direction's illustration style>" --out img/<name>.png` (`--size 1024x1024` square, `1024x1536` tall), all in the one style your direction draws from the product's world; a prompt holds no text to write, no logo, no real person and no real data, and each image gets its alt text. Otherwise draw with CSS or inline SVG. Fill them with believable data in the product's language and words, taken from the source (names, amounts, dates), never lorem ipsum. When you are given the captures of the screens as they are, redraw those screens with what they hold: the same data, the same sections, every action they offer, nothing added and nothing taken away; only the look changes. Keep exactly what the charter fixes, its colours, type and feeling; your angle decides only what it leaves open. Each page holds at the widths given: nothing scrolls sideways, a label stays on one line, cards keep one shape, text is cut only with an ellipsis, and what one taps is big enough. Link the two pages to each other as the product would, by their file names (`index.html`, `<dense>.html`), never from `/`, so they also open as files. Run no git and commit nothing: your folder is not a worktree. Before you reply, name the banned look yours is closest to; if one describes it, start again, unless the charter asks for that look in so many words. Reply in ten lines at most: the two files, then two plain sentences for someone who is not a designer, what one sees, and how it reads the charter or what it is drawn from.
