# /aeom: the directions

Part of the `/aeom` skill: [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) says when to read this file (section 2b, in a run that builds directions). **Briefing any worker** and sections 3 to 5 are in [phase-wave.md](phase-wave.md); everything else it does not say here (section 0, **The cost, before the judges**, **The result page, served**, **The standard of the front**) is in SKILL.md.

## 2b. Directions (in a run without an option once the verdict finds something to redo, or with `/aeom --forcer-directions`)

Instead of fixing the current look, start from new ones and keep the best: three by default, or as many as the user asked with `/aeom --directions <n>`. Each one costs a worker, a worktree and its own app, so more is rarely better.

1. **The sources, from the product.** Run `aeom product --brief`: what kind of product it is, what the app is for, who uses it and its main loop, for a game or a creative product its genre and fiction, the feeling it aims for, its references and its visual assets, as the product sheet says them, and what it leaves out as to confirm. Follow [directions.md](directions.md): list twice as many sources as directions from that brief (for a game or a creative product, from its world, as its section **For a game or a creative product** says), pick as many as directions, far apart, and drop any whose obvious rendering is a banned look. Give each a short slug, such as `nuancier`, and one sentence naming the part of the brief it serves and how, such as « Sert la boucle principale : choisir une pièce, l'ajouter, suivre sa commande, comme on suit un coupon de tissu de la vitrine à l'atelier. » Never a source that rests on what the brief leaves out. If the brief says the kind is not said, the sheet was written before AEOM said it: draft it again first, steps 2 to 5 of section 2c, so it says the kind and, for a game or a creative product, its world, then run the brief again. If the brief exits 1, it says why: a part the user renamed or removed (tell the user which heading to restore, as [product.md](product.md) says, and stop: the sheet is theirs), or nothing confirmed (go back to steps 2 to 5 of section 2c and write what you see, with its source). With `/aeom --forcer-directions` on a project that has no product sheet yet, write it first, before the run branch: steps 1 to 5 of section 2c. Either way, once the sheet is written and before any judge or worker (those of section 1 come first), say what the run will cost and wait for the go, as **The cost, before the judges** says.
2. **The screens a direction is judged on.** Two or three, from the main loop: the screen a user lands on first (the config's `home` when it has one, otherwise `/`), and one or two others, at least one of them dense with interface: a list, a form, a management screen, the one the user works in most. A direction judged on a screen that is mostly one illustration says nothing of how it holds a real screen. Note their routes and the files behind them.
3. **One worker per direction, at once**, each on Sonnet as **Briefing any worker** says. Before each worker starts, give its worktree the project's dependencies as section 0 says (a link to the main checkout's, an install when the bundler refuses it), and a warm build: when the main checkout keeps a build cache (`.next/cache` for Next.js), copy it into the worktree as a clone, which costs almost no disk (`cp -cR` on macOS, `cp -R --reflink=auto` on Linux), so its build starts from the main one's instead of from nothing. Note the run checkout's root first (`MAIN="$(git rev-parse --show-toplevel)"`): every path below that starts with `$MAIN` must stay absolute, because commands run from inside other worktrees. Note too the commit the run starts from, before any direction lands, `KIT_BASE=$(git rev-parse aeom/$RUN)`: section 5 replays the app as it was there and puts files back from it. For each source, create a worktree from the run branch (`$WT/$RUN-dir-<source>`) and launch a worker with the direction brief. Each owns the shared files and the files of the screens of step 2 in its own worktree; they never see each other.
4. **Capture each one.** For each worktree, from inside it, start its app on its own port and capture the screens of step 2, and nothing else: no crawl of the site, no check of every page. Start it as section 0 says for the app. Use the run checkout's CLI, since a fresh worktree has no build:
   `aeom capture --start "<start command with PORT=<port>>" --url http://localhost:<port> --pages <the routes, comma-separated> --widths 1280 --out "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions/<source>"`, then lay its screens side by side in one image, labelled by route, so every judge sees them together: `aeom sheet --out "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions/<source>.png" --columns <how many screens> "<route>=<its capture @1280>"...`. A direction that does not start or does not build gets no capture: say so, and leave it out of the tournament.
5. **Show them.** `aeom sheet --out .aeom/runs/$RUN/directions/sheet.png --columns 1 --width 1600 <source>=<its image of step 4>... --note "<source>=<its sentence>"...` with all of them, the failed ones included, each with its sentence, which reads under its screens. Show the sheet to the user, and each direction's image, with the two plain sentences its worker ended with.
6. **Screen out the banned looks**, only with `--autonome`: without it, the user chooses, and this step and the next are left out (go to step 8). Before the bracket, launch three screening judges at once, each with the sheet and the banned looks from directions.md, and ask each to name the directions one of the banned looks describes. A direction named by two of the three is out; say which and why. If exactly one remains, start the knockout with it alone, `aeom tournament start --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" <it>`: it is the champion without a duel; say so, and go to step 8. If none remains, never crown a banned one: show the user the sheet, say why each was taken out, and ask them as step 8 does whether to build new directions from what they say or to choose one of these themselves (`aeom tournament start --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" <it>`, then `aeom tournament decide`).
7. **Knockout**, only with `--autonome`. `aeom tournament start --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" <the sources still in>`. Then, until there is a champion: `aeom tournament next --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions"` names a duel and its two captures; launch as many duel judges as it says votes are missing, at once, with the duel brief, on the lighter model the session offers: one decides a duel, three the final; when all have replied, record their votes one after another with `aeom tournament vote --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" --duel <n> --voter <v> --winner <source> --reason "<why>"`. Never let the judges write the tournament file themselves: three writers at once lose votes. `aeom tournament status --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions"` shows the bracket and the reasons. Duels of the same round are independent: run them at once.
8. **The user decides.** The second stop of a run, before the waves that cost the most: a direction that misreads the product is the most expensive mistake to undo, and no judge knows the product as its user does, so no judge ranks the directions for them. Unless the user typed `--autonome`, record the directions that built, with no knockout: `aeom tournament start --no-knockout --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" <the sources captured>` (with `--reset` when it holds an earlier round of directions); never run `aeom tournament next` then; show the sheet again, then ask, with the session's question tool when it has one: each direction in its two plain sentences, the banned look it is closest to as its worker said, and what the product sheet guessed about the product's world (its references and feeling marked to confirm), and the question: which one to apply, or none of them. Wait for the answer: nothing goes on without it. Keep it with `aeom tournament decide --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" --choose <source>`, with `--why "<their words>"` when they gave any: it records the decision in the run. When they want none, build new directions from what they said, then ask again. With `--autonome`, steps 6 and 7 choose instead: the judges' champion applies without asking; say so.
9. **Apply the direction decided.** Merge its worktree branch into the run branch (the champion's with `--autonome`): its shared files become the kit and the screens of step 2 are done. Skip the kit wave, and run the screen wave (section 4) on every other page, then measure (section 5) as usual.

**Direction brief.** Give the worker its worktree path, the shared files and the files of the screens of step 2 it may edit, the product brief as `aeom product --brief` printed it, without the part it leaves out, and the app's language, its source and its sentence, the banned looks from directions.md, and the base principles. For a game or a creative product, or whenever the brief lists visual assets, give it the paths of a few of each family. Ask it to:

> Build one art direction for this product from your source, in real code: the shared kit (tokens, header, buttons, states) and these screens rebuilt on it. The direction serves what the product brief says: what kind of product it is, what the app is for, its main loop, and who uses it when the brief says so, as your sentence says; for a game, its world, its genre and the feeling it aims for. Never build on an audience the brief does not give. Take from the source what makes it recognisable (its colours, type, layout conventions, marks) and turn it into a web kit; do not draw a picture of it, and do not imitate a famous site. Open the visual assets you were given before you start: the existing illustrations and logo must look at home in your direction, never pasted onto it. Before you commit, say in your report which banned look yours is closest to and what keeps it apart; if one of them describes it, start again. Keep each screen's content and purpose. Never a new piece of data, business logic or a call to the server, and never a state that claims something happened when nothing did, such as a button that turns to « Ajouté » when nothing was added: what the app cannot do stays as it is. Commit as soon as it works, then check your screens with `aeom check --pages <your screens>` on your own port, never the whole site, and fix what fails. End your report with two plain sentences for someone who is not a designer: what one sees (colours, materials, light, the shape of the blocks and buttons), and what it takes from the product, with no trade word.

**Duel brief.** Give each duel judge the two capture paths, the two source names with their sentences, the product brief without the part it leaves out, the banned looks from directions.md, and the base principles. Each capture holds the screens of step 2 side by side, labelled by route. Ask it to:

> Look at both images, every screen of each. First: if one of the banned looks describes one direction and not the other, the other wins. Then judge on every screen, the dense ones as much as the first: against the base principles; whether you can tell at a glance what kind of product this is (a game looks like a game of its genre, a shop like a shop); whether the existing illustrations, icons and logo look at home in it rather than pasted on; and which serves better what the product brief says (what the app is for, its main loop, its world for a game, and who uses it when the brief says so), is more specific to this product, and less like anything an AI would propose. When the fit with the product, its kind or its assets is what decided, the reason says so. Reply with only a JSON object: `{"winner": "<source>", "reason": "<one sentence naming what you see>"}`.
