# /aeom: the directions

Part of the `/aeom` skill: [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) says when to read this file (section 2b, in a run that builds directions). **Where workers work**, **Briefing any worker** and sections 2 to 6 are in [phase-wave.md](phase-wave.md); everything else it does not say here (section 0, **No cost said**, **The result page, served**, **The standard of the front**) is in SKILL.md.

## 2b. Directions (in a run without an option once the verdict finds something to redo and the person chose new directions, or with `/aeom --forcer-directions`)

Instead of fixing the current look, start from new ones and keep the best: three by default, or as many as the user asked with `/aeom --directions <n>`. Each one costs a worker, a worktree and its own app, so more is rarely better.

1. **The sources, from the product.** Run `aeom product --brief`: what kind of product it is, what the app is for, who uses it and its main loop, for a game or a creative product its genre and fiction, the feeling it aims for, its references and its visual assets, as the product sheet says them, and what it leaves out as to confirm. Follow [directions.md](directions.md): list twice as many sources as directions from that brief (for a game or a creative product, from its world, as its section **For a game or a creative product** says), pick as many as directions, far apart, and drop any whose obvious rendering is a banned look. Give each a short slug, such as `nuancier`, and one sentence naming the part of the brief it serves and how, such as « Sert la boucle principale : choisir une pièce, l'ajouter, suivre sa commande, comme on suit un coupon de tissu de la vitrine à l'atelier. » Never a source that rests on what the brief leaves out. If the brief says the kind is not said, the sheet was written before AEOM said it: draft it again first, steps 2 to 5 of section 2c, so it says the kind and, for a game or a creative product, its world, then run the brief again. If the brief exits 1, it says why: a part the user renamed or removed (tell the user which heading to restore, as [product.md](product.md) says, and stop: the sheet is theirs), or nothing confirmed (go back to steps 2 to 5 of section 2c and write what you see, with its source). With `/aeom --forcer-directions` on a project that has no product sheet yet, write it first, before the run branch: steps 1 to 5 of section 2c. Either way, once the sheet is written and before any judge or worker (those of section 1 come first), say what the run will cost and wait for the go, as **No cost said** says.
2. **The screens a direction is judged on.** Two or three, from the main loop: the screen a user lands on first (the config's `home` when it has one, otherwise `/`), and one or two others, at least one of them dense with interface: a list, a form, a management screen, the one the user works in most. A direction judged on a screen that is mostly one illustration says nothing of how it holds a real screen. Note their routes and the files behind them.
3. **One worker per direction, at once**, each an `aeom-worker` agent (**Briefing any worker**) on this session's own model, passed explicitly to the Agent tool (`model` set to the model this session runs on: the agent's own is Sonnet): a direction is where taste counts most, and there are only a few, the one exception to the Sonnet of **Briefing any worker**. Before each worker starts, give its worktree the project's dependencies as **Where workers work** says (a link to the main checkout's, an install when the bundler refuses it), and a warm build: when the main checkout keeps a build cache (`.next/cache` for Next.js), copy it into the worktree as a clone, which costs almost no disk (`cp -cR` on macOS, `cp -R --reflink=auto` on Linux), so its build starts from the main one's instead of from nothing. Note the run checkout's root first (`MAIN="$(git rev-parse --show-toplevel)"`): every path below that starts with `$MAIN` must stay absolute, because commands run from inside other worktrees. Note too the commit the run starts from, before any direction lands, `KIT_BASE=$(git rev-parse aeom/$RUN)`: section 5 replays the app as it was there and puts files back from it. For each source, create a worktree from the run branch (`$WT/$RUN-dir-<source>`) and launch a worker with the direction brief. Each owns the shared files and the files of the screens of step 2 in its own worktree; they never see each other.
4. **Capture each one.** For each worktree, from inside it, start its app on its own port and capture the screens of step 2, and nothing else: no crawl of the site, no check of every page. Start it as section 0 says for the app. Use the run checkout's CLI, since a fresh worktree has no build:
   `aeom capture --start "<start command with PORT=<port>>" --url http://localhost:<port> --pages <the routes, comma-separated> --widths <the widest of $WIDTHS> --out "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions/<source>"`, then lay its screens side by side in one image, labelled by route, so every judge sees them together: `aeom sheet --out "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions/<source>.png" --columns <how many screens> "<route>=<its capture at the widest of $WIDTHS>"...`. A direction that does not start or does not build gets no capture: say so, and leave it out of the tournament.
5. **Lay them out, with the front as it is.** `aeom sheet --out "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions/now.png" --columns <how many screens> "<route>=<section 1's capture at the widest of $WIDTHS>"...` lays the screens of step 2 as they are today; then `aeom sheet --out "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions/sheet.png" --columns 1 --width 1600 now=<that image> <source>=<its image of step 4>... --note "now=The front as it is today." --note "<source>=<its sentence>"...`, the front as it is first, each direction with its sentence. Show nothing to the user before step 6 has read them.
6. **What a judge sees in them**: one screening judge, an `aeom-judge` agent as every judge, or three at once with `--autonome`, each with the sheet, the banned looks from directions.md and the screening brief below.
   - **Without `--autonome`, nobody is left out**: the person chooses, so every direction that built is shown, and what the judge names goes beside the direction it names, never in its place (« n'écarte rien, c'est à l'utilisateur de choisir »). Show the user the sheet of step 5, and go to step 8.
   - **With `--autonome`, nobody chooses, so the judges screen**: a direction two of the three name is out; say which and why, and lay the sheet out again without it, the front as it is still first (the same `aeom sheet` command as step 5).
     - **Exactly one is left**: start the knockout with it alone, `aeom tournament start --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" <it>`: it is the champion without a duel; say so, and go to step 8.
     - **None is left**: never crown a banned one, and ask nothing. Say why each is out, and improve the front as it is: keep that answer as the bullet **Improve, or new directions** of SKILL.md says (`aeom run mark … --option garder-le-style`, then `aeom verdict --keep-style`), remove the direction worktrees (`git worktree remove "$WT/$RUN-dir-<source>"`, each), and go on with the style kept (step 4 of « Without an option »).
7. **Knockout**, only with `--autonome`. `aeom tournament start --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" <the sources still in>`. Then, until there is a champion: `aeom tournament next --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions"` names a duel and its two captures; launch as many duel judges as it says votes are missing, at once, with the duel brief, each an `aeom-judge` agent: one decides a duel, three the final; when all have replied, record their votes one after another with `aeom tournament vote --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" --duel <n> --voter <v> --winner <source> --reason "<why>"`. Never let the judges write the tournament file themselves: three writers at once lose votes. `aeom tournament status --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions"` shows the bracket and the reasons. Duels of the same round are independent: run them at once.
8. **The user decides.** The second stop of a run, before the waves that cost the most: a direction that misreads the product is the most expensive mistake to undo, and no judge knows the product as its user does, so no judge ranks the directions for them. Unless the user typed `--autonome`, record every direction that built, with no knockout: `aeom tournament start --no-knockout --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" <the sources>` (with `--reset` when it holds an earlier round of directions); never run `aeom tournament next` then; show the sheet again, then ask, with the session's question tool when it has one: each direction in its two plain sentences, what the judge said of it when it named it, the banned look it is closest to as its worker said, and what the product sheet guessed about the product's world (its references and feeling marked to confirm), and the question: which one to apply, keep the front as it is and improve it (its style kept), or none of them. Wait for the answer: nothing goes on without it. Keep it with `aeom tournament decide --dir "$MAIN/.aeom/runs/$RUN/directions" --choose <source>`, with `--why "<their words>"` when they gave any: it records the decision in the run. When they keep the front as it is, keep that answer and remove the direction worktrees as step 6 says for `--autonome`, and the run goes on with the style kept (step 4 of « Without an option »). When they want none, build new directions from what they said, then ask again. With `--autonome`, steps 6 and 7 choose instead: the judges' champion applies without asking; say so.
9. **Apply the direction decided.** A direction kept by `/aeom directions` is replaced: `aeom direction drop`. Merge its worktree branch into the run branch (the champion's with `--autonome`): its shared files become the kit and the screens of step 2 are done. Skip the kit wave, and run the screen wave (section 4) on every other page, then measure (section 5) as usual.

**Direction brief.** Give the worker its worktree path, the shared files and the files of the screens of step 2 it may edit, the product brief as `aeom product --brief` printed it, without the part it leaves out, and the app's language, its source and its sentence, the banned looks and the paragraph on the product's name from directions.md, the base principles, and section 1's captures of the screens of step 2 as they are today: the bar to beat for craft, not a look to copy. For a game or a creative product, or whenever the brief lists visual assets, give it the paths of a few of each family. Ask it to:

> Build one art direction for this product from your source, in real code: the shared kit (tokens, header, buttons, states) and these screens rebuilt on it. The direction serves what the product brief says: what kind of product it is, what the app is for, its main loop, and who uses it when the brief says so, as your sentence says; for a game, its world, its genre and the feeling it aims for. Never build on an audience the brief does not give. Take from the source what makes it recognisable (its colours, type, layout conventions, marks) and turn it into a web kit; do not draw a picture of it, and do not imitate a famous site. Open the visual assets you were given before you start: the existing illustrations and logo must look at home in your direction, never pasted onto it. Before you commit, say in your reply which banned look yours is closest to and what keeps it apart; if one of them describes it, start again. Look at the captures of these screens as they are today: your direction must be at least as polished and as current as them, or start again, and it is a new direction, not theirs redone. The product's name may give one wink that makes sense, only if one comes naturally from your source: a detail, never the look. Keep each screen's content and purpose. Never a new piece of data, business logic or a call to the server, and never a state that claims something happened when nothing did, such as a button that turns to « Ajouté » when nothing was added: what the app cannot do stays as it is. Commit as soon as it works, then check your screens with `aeom check --pages <your screens>` on your own port, never the whole site, and fix what fails. End your reply with two plain sentences for someone who is not a designer: what one sees (colours, materials, light, the shape of the blocks and buttons), and what it takes from the product, with no trade word.

**Screening brief.** Give the judge the sheet, the front as it is first in it, the product brief without the part it leaves out, and the banned looks from directions.md. Ask it to:

> Look at every direction on the sheet, every screen of each, and at the front as it is today, first on the sheet. Name each direction that one of these describes: a banned look describes it as a whole; it looks dated, as the banned looks say; it is less polished, less clear or less current than the front as it is; it is drawn from the product's name rather than from the world of its users, or for a game from its fiction, as directions.md says. For each one you name, one sentence naming what you see. Name none when none deserves it. Reply with only a JSON object: `{"out": {"<source>": "<why>"}}`, empty when every direction can be shown.

**Duel brief.** Give each duel judge the two capture paths, the two source names with their sentences, the product brief without the part it leaves out, the banned looks from directions.md, and the base principles. Each capture holds the screens of step 2 side by side, labelled by route. Ask it to:

> Look at both images, every screen of each. First: if one of the banned looks describes one direction and not the other, the other wins. Then judge on every screen, the dense ones as much as the first: against the base principles; whether you can tell at a glance what kind of product this is (a game looks like a game of its genre, a shop like a shop); whether the existing illustrations, icons and logo look at home in it rather than pasted on; and which serves better what the product brief says (what the app is for, its main loop, its world for a game, and who uses it when the brief says so), is more specific to this product, and less like anything an AI would propose. When the fit with the product, its kind or its assets is what decided, the reason says so. Reply with only a JSON object: `{"winner": "<source>", "reason": "<one sentence naming what you see>"}`.
